# Party Combat Concept

## Core Idea

The player controls a party instead of one character. An MVP can begin with two playable characters so that party cooperation, targeting, support effects, and enemy crowd control can be tested without immediately building a full four-character system.

The main source of fun is creating a party whose members have different strengths and then coordinating their actions during battle. A later version may expand the party to four characters and support more specialized roles.

## Characters and Skills

Each character can equip a selection of active skills. Active skills may require an appropriate equipped weapon, allowing the player to change a character's combat role through loadout choices.

Inherent Skills are passive abilities permanently associated with the character who acquired them. They cannot be transferred between party members. A character may gain an Inherent Skill through an event, a personal experience, or repeated behavior in combat, such as frequently finishing weakened enemies with Slashing attacks.

This combination is intended to preserve player customization while still allowing individual party members to develop distinct identities.

## Equipment and Damage Types

Characters may equip weapons, armor, and accessories. A future version may allow more than one weapon per character and may introduce weapon proficiency, but these features do not need to be complete in the first prototype.

Weapons and magic can use different damage categories. Armor, accessories, enemies, and skills may increase or reduce specific categories, creating reasons to prepare different party members for different encounters.

## Exploration and Events

During room exploration, the game may show only the selected party leader or may show other party members following the leader. This presentation choice remains open and does not need to change the combat rules.

Events may ask the player to choose one or more party members as responders. Possible results include equipment, consumables, resources, Inherent Skills, other benefits, or negative consequences.

## Environment Interaction

Environment interactions are an optional extension rather than a complete required system. Current provisional examples are Oil + Fire and Water + Lightning. Actions may create an environmental state, and later Actions may react with it.

Future room objects may react to general properties such as Fire, Lightning, Slashing, Blunt, Flammable, Conductive, Cuttable, or Fragile. Exact rules and MVP inclusion remain open for team discussion.

## Reserved Ideas

Possible future additions include follow-up attacks, deeper weapon proficiency, more damage categories, character-specific event development, advanced environmental reactions, and selected D&D-inspired combat rules. These ideas are not required for the first party-combat prototype.
